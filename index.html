This project compiles baseline environmental observations from field photographs into a searchable plant registry.

Field Methodology

Photographs are used as the primary visual record. Species identifications are based on visible characteristics; where the photograph does not support species-level certainty, the record uses a genus-level or approximate identification.

Core Deliverables

  • Plant-by-plant registry records
  • Health/condition observations
  • Image-linked documentation
  • Location information where readable from the supplied photographs

Important Data Note

Heights were not invented where no measurement was visible. Likewise, exact GPS values are included only where they were available from the earlier records; the remaining photographs are identified as Mumbai, Maharashtra, India rather than assigning unsupported coordinates.

Plant Conservation

Plant conservation is important for protecting green spaces, maintaining biodiversity and keeping our environment healthy. The plants documented in this project can be conserved through proper care, regular monitoring and responsible environmental practices.

Why Plant Conservation Matters

Plants provide oxygen, shade, food and shelter for many living organisms. They also help maintain soil quality and contribute to a healthy and balanced environment. Protecting existing plants and encouraging responsible planting can help maintain green cover for future generations.

Ways to Conserve Plants

  • Protect existing trees and plants from unnecessary cutting or damage.
  • Provide plants with the appropriate amount of water and avoid unnecessary water wastage.
  • Protect the soil around plants and avoid damaging their roots.
  • Prefer suitable native and locally adapted plant species when planning new plantations.
  • Regularly monitor plants for diseases, pests, physical damage and other changes in their condition.
  • Keep plant-growing areas clean and free from plastic and other waste.
  • Encourage students and community members to participate in tree plantation and green-cover activities.

Our Role as Students

Students can contribute to plant conservation by protecting plants in their surroundings, avoiding unnecessary damage, saving water, maintaining cleanliness and spreading awareness about the importance of green spaces. Regular documentation and observation of plants can also help identify changes in plant health over time.

Conservation Through Documentation

The plant mapping project itself supports conservation by creating a record of the plants present in the selected area. Recording plant names, photographs, condition and location provides useful baseline information that can be referred to during future monitoring and maintenance.

Simple Conservation Checklist

  • 🌱 Protect existing plants.
  • 💧 Use water responsibly.
  • 🌳 Avoid unnecessary cutting.
  • 🪴 Protect soil and plant roots.
  • 📋 Monitor plant health regularly.
  • ♻️ Keep green areas clean.
  • 🌿 Encourage responsible planting.

Plant Conservation Techniques

  • Mulching: Cover the soil around plants with dry leaves, grass or other organic material to retain moisture, reduce weeds and protect plant roots.
  • Drip Irrigation: Provide water slowly and directly to the root area of plants. This reduces water wastage and helps maintain proper soil moisture.
  • Rainwater Harvesting: Collect and store rainwater for watering plants and maintaining green areas, especially during periods of low rainfall.
  • Composting: Convert suitable organic waste such as fallen leaves and plant material into compost. Compost can improve soil quality and provide nutrients to plants.
  • Organic Pest Management: Monitor plants regularly for pests and diseases and use environmentally responsible methods to control them while reducing unnecessary chemical use.
  • Native Planting: Prefer suitable native and locally adapted plants because they are generally better suited to the local climate and environmental conditions.
  • Tree Guards: Use suitable protective barriers around young trees to prevent physical damage from people, animals and other environmental factors.
  • Regular Monitoring: Record changes in plant height, health, condition and surroundings regularly. Early identification of problems can help in taking appropriate care.
  • Proper Pruning: Remove dead, damaged or diseased branches when necessary to support healthy plant growth. Pruning should be done carefully to avoid unnecessary damage.
  • Soil Protection: Avoid excessive digging, soil compaction and damage to roots. Maintaining healthy soil supports better plant growth.
